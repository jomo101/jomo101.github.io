Postdoctoral Researcher, Nuclear Theory · Lattice quantum field theory

jmoscoso@umd.edu INSPIRE arXiv GitHub

I use lattice quantum field theory to study how hadrons and light nuclei emerge from QCD, and I write the simulation and analysis codes that make those calculations possible. A lattice calculation never hands you the quantity you actually want: scattering amplitudes and resonance properties have to be reconstructed from finite-volume correlation functions. That reconstruction — and the statistics it takes to do it honestly — is most of what I do.

Recently: the two-pole structure of the $\Lambda(1405)$, whether di-nucleons bind at heavy pion mass, and formalism for reading the structure of bound states off finite-volume matrix elements.

What I work on

Lattice QFT

Hadron spectroscopy and structure from first principles. Lüscher formalism, coupled-channel scattering.

Scientific computing

Monte Carlo gauge-field generation in C, MPI, Krylov solvers, terabyte-scale campaigns on DOE supercomputers.

Statistical inference

Signal extraction under exponentially bad signal-to-noise. Resampling, correlated fits, model averaging, hierarchical Bayes.

Software

PyCALQ
Correlator analysis through the Lüscher quantization condition. A 440× speedup in the fit engine made $N = 1000$ bootstrap multi-wave fits tractable.
Wilson-clover RHMC gauge-field sampler
A from-scratch lattice QCD sampler in C, MPI-parallel and validated against physics identities. Integrator work cut the energy violation 30-fold at equal solve budget.
